## Overview

Created in 2002, *Burning of the Codices* is a monumental acrylic painting on canvas that represents a significant thematic expansion in Carmen Lomas Garza’s body of work. While the artist is most widely celebrated for her intimate, nostalgic portrayals of everyday Mexican American domestic life and childhood memories in South Texas, this artwork turns its focus toward a devastating moment of historical trauma. The painting depicts the intentional destruction of indigenous Mesoamerican books, known as codices, by Spanish colonizers during the sixteenth century. By engaging with this profound historical event, Garza links the contemporary Chicano experience directly to its indigenous roots, exploring themes of cultural erasure, the loss of ancestral knowledge, and the enduring resilience required to survive centuries of colonization. It is a powerful testament to her role not just as a documentarian of the present, but as a critical historian of her people's past.

## What you see

The composition plunges the viewer into a scene of chaotic devastation and historical violence. Unlike her peaceful domestic interiors, the setting here is an outdoor plaza dominated by a massive, roaring bonfire. Spanish friars, identifiable by their traditional religious habits, are actively throwing intricately painted, accordion-style books—the indigenous codices—into the flames. The fire consumes the colorful pages, sending thick plumes of smoke into the sky. Surrounding the fire are indigenous figures, depicted in states of profound grief, shock, and forced submission. Some are restrained or guarded by armed Spanish soldiers, rendering them helpless to stop the destruction of their sacred and historical texts. Garza’s signature narrative clarity ensures that the tragedy of the event is immediately legible. Despite the violence of the subject matter, she renders the scene with her characteristic flat, precise brushwork and vibrant color palette, drawing the eye to the meticulous details of the codices before they are lost to the fire.

## Cultural and historical context

The event depicted in this painting refers to the systemic campaign of cultural genocide carried out during the Spanish conquest of the Americas. The most infamous of these events occurred in 1562, when Bishop Diego de Landa ordered the burning of countless Maya codices in the Yucatán Peninsula. These codices were the repositories of centuries of indigenous knowledge, containing complex astronomical data, historical records, religious literature, and botanical science. The colonizers viewed these texts as heretical and sought to eradicate them to ease the process of forced conversion to Christianity. For the contemporary Chicano movement, which actively sought to reclaim and honor its indigenous heritage, the burning of the codices represents a primary wound—an act of epistemicide that severed Mexican Americans from a vast portion of their ancestral history. Understanding this loss is crucial to understanding the urgency with which Chicano artists, including Garza, work to preserve their surviving cultural practices today.

## Artist's intent and technique

With *Burning of the Codices*, Garza’s intent shifts from personal recollection to collective, historical mourning. She created this piece to visually educate her audience about the aggressive forces that attempted to erase indigenous identity, providing a historical explanation for the fragmentation of Mexican American history. Her use of acrylic on a large canvas allows for a bold, expansive scene that commands the viewer’s attention, fitting for a subject of such monumental historical gravity. By applying her familiar, accessible *monitos* style to this traumatic event, she brings the distant past into immediate, emotional proximity for the viewer. The detailed rendering of the codices themselves serves as a poignant homage to the indigenous artists and scribes who originally created them, acting as a visual act of solidarity across centuries.

## Why this matters in the exhibition

Within the "Picturing the Familiar" exhibition, *Burning of the Codices* provides vital historical grounding. It serves as the deep, historical foundation upon which Garza’s other works rest. When visitors view her paintings of family gatherings, traditional cooking, and community healing, this artwork reminds them that these surviving traditions are not merely quaint customs; they are the result of profound resilience in the face of centuries of attempted erasure. It elevates the everyday acts of cultural preservation depicted in her other paintings, framing them as urgent, ongoing acts of resistance against the legacy of the fires depicted here. This artwork challenges visitors to consider how history is recorded, who has the power to destroy it, and the vital importance of artists who dedicate their lives to remembering.

## Overview

*Cumpleaños de Lala y Tudi* (Lala and Tudi’s Birthday), created in 1991, is a masterful lithograph by Carmen Lomas Garza. This artwork captures the exuberant, chaotic, and heartwarming atmosphere of a joint family birthday party. Drawing from the artist's own well of childhood memories in South Texas, the piece is a celebration of family milestones, community gathering, and the simple joys of youth. It stands as a testament to Garza's incredible skill in printmaking and her unwavering commitment to narrative art.

## What you see

The composition centers on a lively outdoor gathering taking place in a family's yard. The scene is dense with activity, featuring a large number of figures, mostly children and older relatives, engaged in the festivities. A focal point of the narrative is the breaking of a piñata; children are gathered around, anticipating the shower of candies, while adults watch on with amusement and protective care. The space is adorned with party decorations, and the architecture of the modest home borders the celebration, grounding the event in a specific, recognizable domestic setting. The lithograph technique allows for crisp, defined lines and a rich interplay of light and texture, giving the scene a dynamic, moving quality despite the stillness of the medium. 

## Cultural and historical context

In Mexican American culture, family celebrations like birthdays are vital events that extend far beyond the immediate nuclear family. They are occasions that reinforce community bonds, bringing together grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins, and neighbors. These gatherings, often held in the accessible and welcoming spaces of backyards or front porches, act as informal institutions of cultural preservation. During a time when the Chicano movement was fighting for basic civil rights and dignity in the public sphere, maintaining these strong, joyful community networks in the private sphere was a critical form of cultural endurance. 

## Artist's intent and technique

Garza created this lithograph to document and validate the happy memories of her childhood, countering the negative narratives that frequently surrounded Mexican American communities in the mainstream media. By focusing on a universal experience like a birthday party, she builds a bridge of empathy with viewers from all backgrounds, while maintaining the specific cultural markers of her heritage. Lithography requires immense precision and a deep understanding of drawing; Garza uses this technique to create a composition where every figure has a distinct personality and role within the narrative. Her meticulous rendering ensures that the viewer feels the energy of the party—the laughter, the movement, and the sense of belonging.

## Why this matters in the exhibition

Within "Picturing the Familiar," *Cumpleaños de Lala y Tudi* highlights Garza's mastery of printmaking alongside her painting practice. It emphasizes the exhibition's theme of memory as a communal asset. The artwork shows visitors how Garza acts as a visionary documentarian, capturing the fleeting, beautiful moments of ordinary life and rendering them permanent. It reinforces the idea that her activism is deeply rooted in love and joy, inviting everyone who views it to reflect on the celebrations and gatherings that have shaped their own family histories.

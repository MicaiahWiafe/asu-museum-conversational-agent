## Overview

Created in 1989, *Lessons* is a touching lithograph by Carmen Lomas Garza that highlights the quiet, formative moments of early education and creative discovery within the home. Rather than depicting a formal classroom setting, the artwork centers on the family dining table, transforming a space typically reserved for meals into a vibrant hub of learning and artistic exploration. The piece captures a family of six deeply engaged in the simple, profound act of drawing and painting together. Drawing directly from Garza's own biography—her mother was a self-taught painter who heavily influenced her, and her parents were staunch advocates for education—this lithograph serves as a heartfelt tribute to the familial encouragement of creativity. It beautifully illustrates how the seeds of artistic talent and intellectual curiosity are often planted in the most modest and intimate of domestic spaces.

## What you see

The composition is centered around a large, rectangular table draped with a white cloth, set against a softly colored green wall. Gathered around the table are a mother, a father, and their four children, all intently focused on their individual creative tasks. The table is scattered with the tools of childhood art: blank paper, scattered crayons, and a watercolor paint set. The children are happily coloring and painting; one particularly young girl even stands on her wooden chair just to comfortably reach her paper. The parents are actively participating, observing, and guiding their children's efforts. The room is filled with domestic details that ground the scene: a calendar featuring a woman playing a guitar hangs on the left wall, while a framed picture of a church adorns the right. On the warm wooden floor below, a small toy car and a doll lie temporarily forgotten in favor of the art session above.

## Cultural and historical context

In many working-class Mexican American families, the dining or kitchen table serves a multifunctional purpose. It is the heart of the home—the place where food is prepared, meals are shared, bills are paid, and homework is done. During an era when the public education system often marginalized or failed to adequately support Spanish-speaking or bicultural children, the home became the primary institution for instilling confidence, cultural pride, and intellectual curiosity. Garza’s depiction of this family art session pushes back against harmful stereotypes that framed Mexican American households as lacking in educational ambition. Instead, she presents a reality where parents actively invest time and attention into nurturing their children's creative and cognitive development, ensuring that the next generation has the tools to express themselves and succeed.

## Artist's intent and technique

Garza created this lithograph to document and validate the informal, yet crucial, educational practices that occur within the family unit. Her intent is to honor parents who foster a love of learning, specifically acknowledging the role her own parents played in her journey to becoming a professional artist. By choosing lithography for this piece, Garza utilizes a printmaking technique that relies on drawing directly onto a stone or metal plate. This medium is perfectly suited for the subject matter, as the crisp, illustrative lines echo the very act of drawing that the family is engaged in. The gentle, muted color palette applied to the print enhances the nostalgic and tender mood of the scene, allowing the viewer to focus on the intimate interactions and the shared concentration of the figures.

## Why this matters in the exhibition

Within the ASU Art Museum’s retrospective, *Lessons* provides a critical biographical and thematic touchstone. It offers visitors a visual explanation of Garza’s own artistic origins, connecting her monumental career back to the simple act of a child coloring at the kitchen table. The artwork underscores the exhibition’s focus on the intergenerational transmission of knowledge and values. It reminds viewers that the preservation of culture and the fostering of talent are not just the purview of schools or museums, but are daily, loving acts performed by parents. This piece invites visitors to reflect on their own early memories of learning and creativity, fostering a deep, empathetic connection with the artist's personal history.
